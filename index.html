Treat math

Halloween candy goes wrong in three places: the door count guessed from vibes instead of from the houses on the route, the piece count that ignores the labeled three-a-kid rule, and the bulk bag bought by hope instead of by arithmetic. Treat math turns the street into the kids, turns the kids into the pieces, and turns the bags into the leftovers. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Kid plan - the houses on the route at a labeled lit rate and a labeled knock size become the kids at your door.
  • Treat plan - the kids at a labeled pieces-each become the pieces and the bulk bags to buy.
  • Leftover plan - the bags bought minus the pieces served become the pieces staying home.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 70 percent of houses lit, 1.5 kids a knock, 3 pieces a kid and 250 pieces a bulk bag - all illustrative defaults you can change by counting. Last year's actual door count beats any forecast, and a labeled non-food bowl keeps teal-pumpkin visitors included. This is planning arithmetic, not a census.
